# ADR 0006: A RiC-native entity model; Heratio is the floor, RiC the measure

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-20
- Deciders: Dr Johan Pieterse
- Gate: G2

## Context

Dr Johan Pieterse, 20 October 2026: "Add what Heratio does not have and do not follow Heratio verbatim. We need RiC 100%." Heratio's functionality is the minimum the Workspace must offer; its data model, AtoM's, is not the model. Where AtoM keeps names, dates, places and relations as text or terms, RiC-CM 1.0 and RiC-O 1.1 make them entities and reified relations. Authority records, repositories and archival descriptions are built on this model, and so is everything after them.

Decisions for authority records (20 October 2026): evidence and assertions on relations are built in from the start; external authority lookups (Wikidata, VIAF, ULAN, LCNAF, ISNI) are parked, not discarded; AI name recognition is out of scope.

## Decision

Every term below is defined in RiC-O 1.1 unless it says otherwise; a gap goes to openric/spec as an issue before the Workspace relies on its own term (ADR 0003, rule 2).

1. **Entities.** Record resources (`rico:RecordSet`, `rico:Record`, `rico:RecordPart`); agents as `rico:Person`, `rico:Family`, `rico:CorporateBody`, `rico:Position` and `rico:Mechanism` (all `rico:Agent`); repositories as corporate bodies that hold records; `rico:Place`; `rico:Concept` for subjects and other access points; later `rico:Activity`, `rico:Rule`, `rico:Mandate`, `rico:Instantiation`. Each has its own persistent IRI.
2. **Appellations are entities, not columns.** A name is a `rico:Name` (`rico:AgentName` for an agent, `rico:PlaceName` for a place) with its language, the dates it was used (`rico:usedFromDate`, `rico:usedToDate`) and a name type (authorised, parallel, standardised, other). RiC-O 1.1 has no property for the name type: proposed to openric/spec as an extension term (openric/spec#64). An identifier is a `rico:Identifier` with a `rico:IdentifierType` (`rico:hasIdentifierType`).
3. **Dates are entities.** A `rico:Date` with its written form (`rico:expressedDate`), a normalised value (`rico:normalizedDateValue`, ISO 8601 or EDTF), a qualifier (`rico:dateQualifier`, such as circa) and a type (`rico:hasDateType`, from the OpenRiC date-type vocabulary). An entity's dates are linked to it by the RiC-O property for their meaning (`rico:hasBeginningDate`, `rico:hasEndDate`, `rico:hasCreationDate`, `rico:isAssociatedWithDate` and the others). The record dates of v0.0.7 move into this model.
4. **Relations are reified RiC relations.** One table holds every relation between two entities: source, target, a relation type that names its `rico:Relation` subclass and shortcut property (such as `rico:AgentHierarchicalRelation`, `rico:FamilyRelation`, `rico:CreationRelation`), a role (`rico:creationWithRole` or `openricx:relationHasAgentRole`, role-type vocabulary), its dates (`rico:relationHasDate`), context (`rico:relationHasContext`), state (`rico:relationState`), certainty (`rico:relationCertainty`) and source of information (`rico:relationSource`). A record resource that proves a relation is linked to it with `rico:evidences`.
5. **Assertions from the start (WS-006).** A relation is held through one or more assertions: who asserted it (an account, an import or a model), when, on what basis, with what review status (a concept), and which earlier assertion it supersedes. Competing assertions about the same relation coexist; superseding keeps the old one. A machine assertion exports with the Inferred-Provenance markers (extension ontology 1.4.0). This goes beyond Heratio, by decision.
6. **Access points are relations to entities.** Subjects are `rico:Concept` entities (`rico:hasOrHadSubject`); places are `rico:Place` entities with their own names, dates and hierarchy; occupations are `rico:OccupationType` concepts (`rico:hasOrHadOccupationOfType`). Institutions add and edit them; they are not free text.
7. **Heratio is the floor.** Every function of the corresponding Heratio module is present, in the Workspace's own screens. A function Heratio does not have, or a data structure RiC needs that AtoM lacks, is added and recorded here or in a later ADR.

## Consequences

The ISAD(G), ISAAR(CPF) and ISDIAH screens stay organised by their standards, but what they write is RiC: a field such as "dates of existence" writes `rico:Date` entities and the relation that connects them. Export to OpenRiC JSON-LD becomes a direct reading of the store. The cost is more tables and more joins than AtoM's flat model, and a migration of the v0.0.7 record dates.

## Implementation notes

- **v0.0.10, dates.** One `dates` table for every entity; the date-property vocabulary names the RiC-O property of each link; the normalised value is EDTF, with its earliest and latest day derived for sorting.
- **v0.0.11, names and identifiers.** Name elements of the ISAD(G) and ISDIAH forms (`config workspace.name_elements`) write `rico:Name` entities; the name type waits on openric/spec#64. One authorised name per language and one local identifier per entity are enforced in the database.
- **v0.0.12, relations and assertions.** Relation types are checked against the RiC-O 1.1 ontology file: each concept names its Relation subclass, `_role` property and shortcut. `config workspace.relation_types` says which kinds each connects, as source and target pairs, in the direction of the shortcut, whichever page the relation is made from. A person's assertion is accepted as made; an import's or a model's is proposed until a person reviews it. A relation's standing is derived from its current assertions: accepted, disputed, proposed or rejected. Assertions are never edited, only superseded.
- **Kept as columns, exported as relations.** The arrangement of units (`parent_id`, exported as `rico:hasOrHadPart`) and the current holding repository (`repository_id`, `rico:hasOrHadHolder`) stay columns: the cycle and ordering guards depend on them. Former holders and other custody are relations.
- **Context.** `rico:relationHasContext` is stored and checked in the database. It has no screen until activities exist, because an activity is what a context usually is.
- **v0.0.13, places and concepts.** An institution's own vocabularies live in the vocabulary tables, with the institution, a kind from the concept-scheme-kind vocabulary (subjects, occupation types, place types, documentary form types) and the RiC-O class each kind exports as (`config workspace.scheme_kinds`). A local concept has the institution's IRI, labels and notes per language, alternative and hidden labels, a broader concept in its vocabulary, related concepts and matches to other vocabularies (SKOS mapping properties). Heratio's term functions are all there, with one deliberate difference: a concept is never deleted. Deleting is deprecation, and merging deprecates the duplicate with `dcterms:isReplacedBy`, so every IRI and old assertion stays readable. SKOS import gives each concept the institution's IRI and keeps the one it had as an exact match, which also makes importing again an update.
- **Places.** A `rico:Place` entity with `rico:PlaceName` names, a place type, WGS 84 coordinates (`rico:Coordinates`), dates, identifiers (a gazetteer identifier type for GeoNames, TGN or Wikidata) and a description (`rico:generalDescription`, `rico:history`). The place it lies in now is a column (`rico:isOrWasContainedBy`), with the same cycle guard as units of description; containment with dates, such as a former one, is a relation (`rico:containsOrContained`). Like a repository, a place is marked deleted, never removed, and not while it contains places or takes part in relations.
- **Access points.** A subject is a relation from a unit of description to a concept, a place or an agent (`rico:hasOrHadSubject`; RiC-O 1.1 has no Relation subclass for it, so a reified one is a `rico:Relation`). A place access point is a `rico:PlaceRelation` (`rico:isPlaceAssociatedWith`). Both carry assertions like every relation, and a subject or place not yet known is made from the relation screen. Places and local vocabularies are governed by the vocabulary permissions, as Heratio governs places as terms; any account of the institution reads them.
- **Not yet, by design.** Occupation types are linked to persons (`rico:hasOrHadOccupationOfType`) with authority records. Documentary form types are linked to units of description with their completion: `rico:hasDocumentaryFormType` applies to a Record or Record Part only, and the record-set case is settled there. Heratio's community protocol labels on terms (TK and BC labels with an access condition) are access rules, and come with `rico:Rule`. Heratio's converse term and self-reciprocal flags describe relation-type taxonomies, which here are bundled vocabularies.
- **v0.0.15, deleting.** One rule for every entity (`EntityDeletion`): the entity is marked deleted and keeps its IRI. Its relations, access points and uses as evidence go with it. Units inside a unit, units a repository holds and places inside a place block the delete until they are moved or deleted.
- **v0.0.16, authority records.** An agent is one entity table with an agent type (agent-type vocabulary: person, family, corporate body, position, mechanism; the last two are RiC-CM's, not ISAAR(CPF)'s).
  - **Elements:** ISAAR(CPF) elements in the description-element vocabulary write `rico:AgentName` names and language values. Existence, birth and death are Date entities, birth and death for persons only.
  - **Narrative and structure:** places, legal statuses (a new legal-status vocabulary kind) and a person's occupations (`rico:hasOrHadOccupationOfType`, exported with its dates as `openricx:Occupation`) are relations; their narrative elements stay text.
  - **Relations between agents:** 19 relation types follow the RiC-O 1.1 subclasses (family, child, descendance, sibling, spouse, membership, leadership, position holding, position in a group, subdivision, knowing, knowing of, correspondence, teaching, study, work) and the extension ontology's guardianship, adoption and fostering. Each is limited to the agent types its RiC-O domain and range allow, written as `agent:person` and the like in `config workspace.relation_types`.
  - **Permissions and type:** authority records have their own permission kind. The migration grants it to existing institutions' default roles. A type of entity changes only while no relation or birth or death date depends on it.
  - **Merge and split:** merging moves relations, names (as other forms), identifiers (a local one as previous) and contact points, and leaves the duplicate marked deleted and pointing to the agent kept. Splitting moves chosen names and relations to a new agent.
  - **Duplicates and embargo:** duplicate detection compares normalised names within an agent type, and a dismissed pair is not offered again. An embargo date hides a record from those who may not update it.
  - **Dashboard and export:** a dashboard and work queue check the ISAAR(CPF) essential elements. EAC-CPF 2010 export covers persons, families and corporate bodies.
- **Not in v0.0.16, by design.**
  - Functions (ISDF) come with activities.
  - Digital objects on agents come with digital objects.
  - Heratio's external lookups (Wikidata, VIAF, ULAN, LCNAF) stay parked and its NER is out of scope, as decided on 20 October.
  - Subject access points on agents are not offered: RiC-O 1.1 gives subjects only to record resources, so a topic of an agent is a unit about it, or its occupations and functions.
  - Heratio's parent actor and per-field configuration screen have no counterpart; agent hierarchy is a relation.
- **v0.0.17, archival description (first of the remaining releases).**
  - **Publication workflow.** Publication moves the state of each value and name: review, publish, withdraw. It works on a unit alone or with the units inside it, as AtoM's update-publication-status does, and the same way for repositories, places and authority records. Repositories, vocabularies and places, and authority records gain the publish permission.
  - **Required names.** The database keeps an authorised name on every unit, repository, place and agent that is not deleted, with a constraint trigger checked at commit.
  - **Description control.** Units get a description identifier, status and level of detail, and ISAD(G) 3.4.3 is added.
  - **Pages.** Browse searches every level. There are a hierarchy view, an inventory and a finding aid, and print shows one unit. Calculate-dates sets a unit's creation date to the span of the units inside it.
  - **Derived, not stored.** The full reference code comes from the repository code and the identifiers down the hierarchy; an identifier that already includes the code above it is taken as written. A unit's page shows its creators with the history from their authority records (ISAAR(CPF) 5.2.2 feeding ISAD(G) 3.2.2), custody in time order, and a citation. Every entity has a modification history read from the audit log.
- **v0.0.18, export of archival description.**
  - **One rule for every format.** Every export reads the store through one view (`App\Exchange\ExportView`). It gives published values and names only, unless the account may see the drafts of every unit exported and asks for them. Only accepted or disputed relations are exported, and only those whose other end the account may read: an embargoed authority record stays out, and so does a proposed import assertion. A value is given in the language asked for, or else in the language the unit was first described in.
  - **Formats.** EAD 2002 and EAD3 finding aids of a unit and every unit inside it. Each element carries its ISAD(G) number as `encodinganalog`, and creators, access points and associated units carry their persistent IRIs. CSV in AtoM's ISAD(G) columns, with four Workspace columns for what AtoM has no place for, optionally one row per language. Dublin Core (oai_dc) and MODS 3.8 of one unit, with MODS dates in EDTF. A CSV of every unit of the institution, or of one repository.
  - **Checked against the published schemas.** Each XML format is validated in the tests against copies of the official schemas bundled in `resources/schemas`.
  - **Left for later, by decision.** OpenRiC JSON-LD (RiC-O) export belongs to the RiC graph work package, where it is derived from the same store (ADR 0001, WS-010). METS, MARC and PROV-O need digital objects or are library formats.
- **v0.0.19, the RiC term of every field.** Every field on the description forms and pages shows the RiC-O 1.1 term (or extension ontology term) it is kept or exported as, linked to the term's definition: each description element, name, identifier, date, relation and its sub-fields, and the class of each entity (a unit's level, an agent's type, rico:CorporateBody for a repository, rico:Place for a place). The term is data. It is read from the scope note of the Workspace vocabulary concept, as the element's standard number already is, and every element now names one. A test checks every term named against bundled term lists of RiC-O 1.1 and the extension ontology 1.4.0. Where RiC-O 1.1 has no term (description control, contact points, embargo), the field says so. Corrections: a repository's names are rico:AgentName (a repository is a rico:CorporateBody), not rico:Name.
- **v0.0.21, import of archival description (WS-015).**
  - **Formats.** CSV in AtoM's ISAD(G) columns (the columns the export writes, so a file goes out and comes back), and EAD 2002 or EAD3, with or without the namespace, read element by element as the reverse of the export.
  - **Dry run first.** A dry run is the import itself in a transaction that is rolled back, so the report states what importing does. The report lists what each unit becomes and every field that is not taken as given: not imported, left out, ambiguous, created, or a note. Once imported, a reconciliation checks that every unit reported is present with its title and keyed to its source.
  - **Repeatable.** A unit is matched by its persistent IRI, then by its key in the same source (an import_keys row: an AtoM legacyId, an EAD component id or path), then, when asked, by identifier under the same parent. Importing again updates and never duplicates. An empty field never removes a value.
  - **Links.** Creators, subjects, places and names are linked to the entity the file names by IRI, or to the one entity of that name. Two of that name is ambiguous and not linked. None is created where the account may and asked to be, or else left out and listed. The relations an import makes are proposed until a person accepts them; imported values are drafts until published.
  - **One rule set.** Everything is written through the services the screens use, so permissions, database checks and the audit log apply. A unit that fails is not imported, with the units inside it; the rest go on.
  - **Vocabulary.** Collection, the level AtoM and EAD use beside the ISAD(G) levels, is added (rico:RecordSet).
- **v0.0.22, activities (WS-003; the V0.1 demonstration steps 5 to 7).**
  - **The entity.** An activity is a rico:Activity with its own persistent IRI ({base}/activity/{uuid}), names, values, dates, identifiers and relations, marked deleted and never removed, and keeping an authorised name.
  - **Types.** RiC-O 1.1 has one class, so the kind is an activity type (rico:hasActivityType). It comes from the OpenRiC activity-type vocabulary (production, accumulation, custody, transfer, publication, reproduction) or from one of the institution's own activity-type vocabularies, such as one holding Marriage (mapping section 6.5). The database refuses any other concept.
  - **Elements.** Numbered after ISDF where ISDF has the element: names (5.1.2 to 5.1.4), description, history and legislation (5.2.2 to 5.2.4).
  - **Relations.**
    - Performance (rico:PerformanceRelation), with the agent's role.
    - Participation (rico:hasOrHadParticipant), with a role such as spouse or witness from the OpenRiC role-type vocabulary.
    - Result (rico:resultsOrResultedIn): a production activity names what it resulted in.
    - Documentation (rico:ActivityDocumentationRelation).
    - Part of an activity (rico:hasOrHadSubevent).
    - Places and subjects also take activities.
  - **Assertions and evidence.** Every relation of an activity carries assertions and evidence like any other, so a corrected witness is a new assertion that supersedes the old one, which is kept.
  - **Permissions.** Activities are context entities like agents, so the authority permissions govern them. An activity is made on the spot from the relation screen.
- **v0.0.23, the OpenRiC read API and RiC-O JSON-LD (Core Discovery 0.6.0, WS-009 and WS-010 in part).**
  - **The API.** `/api/ric/v1` serves the ten Core Discovery endpoints to anyone. The service description declares that profile only; no endpoint of another profile is served, and every error is RFC 7807 problem details. The API serves the institution whose base IRI has the request's host, or the only institution installed.
  - **Default deny, decided once.** The API reads as the institution's Public role (ADR 0005) through the same view the exports use (`App\Exchange\ExportView`). An entity appears only once its name or title is published and the Public role may read it. An embargoed authority record never appears, and of what appears, only published values are sent.
  - **The RiC-O.** `App\Api\JsonLd` writes the mapping of spec 0.53.0:
    - titles and names with their language tags;
    - dates as openricx:DateRange with literal endpoints typed as precisely as the EDTF value, and a date-type concept;
    - holders, creators and the units above and inside as stubs with their persistent IRIs;
    - the level as rico:hasRecordSetType.
  - **Persistent IRIs resolve.** {base}/record, /agent and /repository/{uuid} answer 303 with the JSON-LD, or the page for someone signed in.
  - **Checked.** The tests validate every response against the specification's JSON Schemas and check every rico: and openricx: term against RiC-O 1.1 and extension ontology 1.4.0. On a rehearsal of live, the conformance probe passed 11 of 11, and the output conformed to the Core Discovery and main SHACL shapes.
  - **Mapping alignment.** Three element notes now follow the specification's mapping: extent (rico:hasExtent), the archivist's note (openricx:descriptiveNote) and the publication note (openricx:publicationInformation).
  - **Departures.** Where the profile, schemas and probe disagree, conformance/README.md records how the Workspace answers.
- **v0.0.24, harvesting and RiC-O export (Export-Only 0.9.0, WS-010).**
  - **OAI-PMH 2.0** at `/api/ric/v1/oai`: all six verbs, by GET or POST, with the protocol's own error envelope.
    - Records come as `oai_dc` or `rico_ld`, the RiC-O JSON-LD in CDATA inside the reference service's wrapper.
    - A record's datestamp is the latest change to it or its parts, so publishing a value is harvested again.
    - Sets are hierarchical: `records`, and `records:{UUID}` for each fonds.
    - Lists come 100 at a time with a resumption token.
    - deletedRecord is no; deleted units are kept, so tombstones can follow.
  - **Per-record export** at `/api/ric/v1/records/{UUID}/export`: the record with its holder and creators described in full, as a JSON-LD `@graph`. Turtle and RDF/XML are made from it by an RDF library (EasyRdf with ml/json-ld), so all three carry one triple set.
  - **Shared visibility.** `App\Api\PublicCatalogue` decides what the public may find, for the read API and for OAI-PMH alike.
- **v0.0.25, instantiations and digital files (WS-007; the V0.1 demonstration step 8).**
  - **The entity.** An instantiation is a rico:Instantiation of a unit of description (rico:isOrWasInstantiationOf), with its own persistent IRI, a title (rico:title), values (description, production technique, physical characteristics, quality of representation, conditions of access, rights and conditions of use), dates and identifiers. It also has a carrier type: a rico:CarrierType concept, so the Workspace does not repeat the specification's known issue K-13. It is read and changed as its unit is, and the access layer gives it the unit's scopes.
  - **Files.** A digital instantiation has at most one master, the file as received, and a reference copy and a thumbnail made from images and from a PDF's first page, without the master's embedded metadata. On upload the SHA-256 is taken and compared with one the uploader gives, the type is read from the content, and ClamAV scans the file. Each file is kept by its checksum outside the web root, and the database refuses any change to a file's content, place or use.
  - **Delivery.** There is one rule, `InstantiationService::deliverable()`:
    - each use needs its read permission on the unit (read-master, read-reference, read-thumbnail);
    - an instantiation on request goes only to those who may update the unit, and a withdrawn one to nobody;
    - files are sent with their type, nosniff, a sandboxing Content-Security-Policy, Cache-Control private and a SHA-256 Digest.
  - **Fixity.** `workspace:fixity` rehashes every file, records and audits a failure, and exits with failure. A passing check is recorded but not audited.
  - **Deletion.** A unit with instantiations cannot be deleted until they are.
  - **Specification 0.54.0.** The Workspace is repinned to it and declares core-discovery 0.7.0 and export-only 0.10.0. Digital Object Linkage is not declared until its endpoints are served.
- **v0.0.26, public discovery (WS-009; the V0.1 demonstration steps 9 and 10).**
  - **The catalogue.** `/discover` needs no account and is the front page for anyone not signed in. It holds a search across archival descriptions, people, families and organisations, activities, places and repositories, and a page for each.
  - **The rules.** Everything reads through `App\Api\PublicCatalogue`, as the read API and OAI-PMH do. An entity appears once its name or title is published and the institution's Public role may read it. Only published values and relations that stand are shown, and anything else is a 404.
  - **Files.** A unit's digital copies reach the public through the same delivery rule as for an account, read as the Public role, so by default reference copies and thumbnails but not masters, and only once the instantiation's title is published. Publishing a unit now publishes its instantiations with it.
  - **The graph.** Each page opens a relationship graph, one or two steps out: relations that stand, the unit above and the units inside, and the holder. It is drawn on the server as SVG with no JavaScript, every node typed by its RiC-O class and linked to its own page, and given again as a list of statements for anyone who does not read the drawing.
  - **Checks.** The public pages pass axe at WCAG 2 AA in light and dark mode.
  - **Persistent IRIs.** A browser not signed in is sent to the public page, a client asking for data to the JSON-LD, and someone signed in to the Workspace page.
- **Still to come for archival description:** physical storage, the DACS, RAD, DC and MODS templates, bulk edit, and links to digital objects and rights. Each comes with its work package in the estimate of 20 October.
