# ADR 0007: API keys, and proposals an archivist reviews

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-26
- Deciders: Dr Johan Pieterse
- Gate: G2

## Context

The AHG Archives MCP (ArchiveHeritageGroup/ahg-archives-mcp) lets AI assistants search and read a catalogue with a citable link for every answer, and lets staff propose improvements that an archivist reviews. It reads the Workspace's open API today, read-only. For the rest of what it does it needs four things the Workspace lacks: API keys tied to an account, an endpoint to post proposals to, a review queue, and a list of a caller's proposals. Its contract is the one Heratio implemented in v1.160.0 to v1.163.3 (heratio#1558): keys with scopes, an end date, hourly and daily limits, last use and revocation; proposals of names, places and dates with evidence and sources, of field text in a language, of relationships between agents, and of transcriptions; answers of queued, already proposed, 403, 429 with Retry-After and 501.

The roadmap had put integration protocols and AI assistance at V2.0 (WS-018, WS-019). On 26 October 2026 Dr Johan Pieterse decided to bring this work forward, to copy Heratio's implementation under the provenance ledger, to hold proposed relationships as assertions, and to include file text and images.

## Options

1. A separate proposals table for everything, as Heratio has (`ahg_ai_suggestion`, `ahg_ner_entity`, relationship proposals). Copies Heratio closely, but puts a second, unreviewed copy of relations beside the relations table, outside WS-006.
2. Proposed relationships as relation assertions with the status Proposed, and a proposals table only for what has no assertion model yet (names, places, dates, values, transcriptions). Uses what WS-006 already built: who asserted, from what source, on what basis, with what review status; a rejected assertion is kept.

## Decision

1. **Keys** (implemented first, v0.0.46). A key belongs to one account of one institution and works only on that institution's host. Only its SHA-256 and first eight characters are kept; the key is shown once. Its scopes are concepts of the `api-scope` vocabulary: read, and write (propose changes). An account may give its keys only the scopes it qualifies for (`workspace.api_scope_requires`: write needs create or update on descriptions or authority records), and a key's scopes are cut to what its account holds at each request, so a key never does more than its account. API reads see what the Public role sees, with or without a key: the MCP's service key must not see more than a visitor. A key has an hourly limit (1,000 unless set) and an optional daily limit; past either the API answers 429 with Retry-After. A revoked key keeps its row. An account manages its own keys; an administrator (manage accounts) manages those of another account of the institution. Issuing, the scopes given and revoking are in the audit log.
2. **Proposals** (implemented second, v0.0.47). `POST /api/ric/v1/records/{key}/proposals` with a write key, of an account that may update the record; a record the public may not see is found only by an account that may see its drafts. Heratio's payload is kept: `entities` (PERSON, ORG, GPE, DATE, with evidence, sources, what is known of a person or organisation, and an existing agent or place proposed for it as `agent_id` or `place_id`), `fields` (Heratio's field names or any ISAD(G) element, with a BCP 47 language; another language than the record's is a translation), `transcripts` (of an instantiation of the record) and `relationships`, with `source` naming what proposed them. A malformed part refuses the whole request with 400, before anything is written. The answer lists what was queued and how much was already waiting (201 when anything was queued, otherwise 200). A proposed relationship becomes a relation with an assertion of status Proposed and origin Model, which names the account too, with the evidence, date and sources as its basis and the record as its evidence (option 2). Both of its agents must be authority records of the archive; a relationship naming someone who is not, or that its type cannot connect, is answered queued false with the reason, and the rest go on. Relation types are named by notation or by how they read, either way round. Names, places, dates, values and transcripts are rows of `proposals`, of a kind from the `proposal-kind` vocabulary and a status from `assertion-status`, until reviewed. No 501 is answered: every kind of the contract is supported.
3. **Review queue** (implemented third, v0.0.48). Proposals to review, under Manage, for those who hold `review-proposals` on the record and may change it; a relation proposed through the API needs the right to change the entity it comes from. Each proposal shows who proposed it and through what, its evidence, sources and confidence; the reviewer accepts it, as it is or with its text, language or link changed, or rejects it, each with a note. Accepting writes through the existing services, as the reviewer, so the change is audited and is a draft until published: a value or translation becomes the record's value of that element in that language; a transcript the instantiation's new Transcript element (`instantiation-transcript`, kept as `openricx:transcript`, proposed in openric/spec#65 with the question whether `rico:generalDescription` should serve instead; RiC-O 1.1 has no property for it, so it is not exported until the spec decides); a person or organisation, or a place, an access point of the record (a `subject` relation to the agent, a `place` relation from the place) to the proposed or a new authority record or place, the new one given what the proposer knew of it; a date a date associated with the record, with an EDTF value if the reviewer gives one. Creating an authority record or place needs the right to. Proposed relations are accepted or rejected with the relation's own review. Nothing reaches a record without that review. The proposer sees each outcome and note in `GET /proposals`.
4. **The caller's proposals** (v0.0.47): `GET /api/ric/v1/proposals?mine=1|0&status=&count=&start=`, with any key; mine=0 only for those who hold `review-proposals`. Statuses are proposed, accepted and rejected; Heratio's pending and approved are accepted as their names.
5. **File text and images**: the text of a file (OCR or transcription) and its images through the API, under the same file access rules as the public catalogue.
6. **Heratio code reused** under the provenance ledger, from heratio-dev at `5100c0f92`: `ApiKeyService`, `ApiAuthenticate`, `ApiRateLimit` and `KeyPageController` of `ahg-api` (ported), and `ProposalController` (ported as `App\Proposals\ProposalService`). The review screens of `ahg-ai-services` are followed for behaviour only (`App\Proposals\ReviewService` and the queue are the Workspace's own): their data access is AtoM's schema (prohibited-copy item 3).

## Evidence

`tests/Feature/ApiKeyTest.php`: scopes no wider than the account's, and cut when it loses a role; only a fingerprint kept and none in the audit log; keyed reads equal to anonymous ones; unknown, revoked, expired and disabled-account keys refused with 401; a key refused on another institution's host; the daily and hourly limits answering 429 with Retry-After; the key pages, including that one account cannot revoke another's key. `tests/Feature/ProposalTest.php`: names, places, dates, values and transcripts queued once and the record unchanged; a malformed part refusing the whole request; a relationship as a proposed assertion with the record as evidence, read either way round and skipped when proposed again; a write key of an account that may update the record required; the caller's list and everyone's for reviewers only; `review-proposals` given to existing roles without giving back a grant an institution took away. `tests/Feature/ProposalReviewTest.php`: a value and a translation written as drafts as the reviewer edited them, and a reviewed proposal not reviewed again; a transcript and a date; a person, an organisation and a place becoming access points to the proposed or a new record; the queue only for reviewers, accepting, rejecting with a note and accepting a relation, and the proposer seeing the outcomes.

## Consequences

The MCP can identify its callers and limit them before proposals exist; reads are unchanged for everyone. Proposals arrive as data a person must review, never as changes. Keys are counted per hour in `api_key_usage`, which grows by at most one row per key and hour; pruning old rows is left until it matters. If the Workspace ever lets a key read drafts, this ADR is the place that must change, because the MCP's leak test relies on keys reading as the public.
