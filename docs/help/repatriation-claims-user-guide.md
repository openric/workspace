# Repatriation claims, dialogue and virtual return

A repatriation claim records that a community, institution or nation asks for an object the institution holds, and where the conversation about it stands. It is a documented request and its status. It is not a legal determination, not a finding of wrongful removal, and not advice. Origin, ownership and lawful-transfer history are for the communities, the holding institution and qualified staff to work out together, case by case, under the law that applies.

The work belongs to accounts that hold **Manage repatriation claims**. Administrators hold it from the start; give it to others under **Admin**, then **Roles**. Everyone who holds it is told of each new claim.

## Registering a claim

Open the description the claim is about and find **Repatriation claims** on its page. **Register a repatriation claim** opens the form:

- **Claimant community or nation**, as the claimant names itself, and if the institution keeps an authority record for that community, the record too.
- **Place / region of origin**, in words, and the place it is, if there is one.
- **Current holder.** Left empty, it is the repository the description names.
- **Stage of dialogue**, Registered for a new claim.
- **Documented evidence**, a factual summary of what the claim rests on.
- **Contact** and **Contact email**. The email is where the claimant is told of the claim and each change of its status.
- **Notes**, for staff.

A claim stays with its description. **Edit the claim** changes everything else.

## Where a claim stands

Each status is a neutral stage, never an outcome:

- **Registered** - recorded and waiting for review.
- **Under review** - the claim and its evidence are being examined.
- **Acknowledged** - the institution has acknowledged the claim and the dialogue is open.
- **Virtual return** - the object is made accessible in its origin context digitally, whatever happens to it physically.
- **Returned** - a physical return has been recorded.
- **Disputed** - the facts or the claim are contested and still being discussed.

The statuses are a vocabulary, so the institution can relabel them in the Dropdown manager. Every change is kept with who made it, when, and the note given with it; a note can be added without changing the status. Those who manage claims are told by mail of each change, except the one who made it, and the claimant is told at the claim's email. Under **Repatriation claims** in the menu, the institution can turn these messages off.

## The dialogue

The claim's page holds the conversation between the institution and the claimant. A member of staff writes for the institution or as a mediator, and marks each message:

- **Shared on the joint record** - the claimant sees it.
- **Internal (staff only)** - the claimant never does.

The dialogue is between named parties and is not moderated.

## The shared record

**Make a link to the shared record** gives a claimant's representative a way in without an account. Choose who it is for and as what: a claimant, a mediator, or an observer, who may only read. A link may be read-only for anyone, and may end on a date.

The link is shown once, at the top of the page, when it is made. Copy it then and send it: the Workspace keeps only a fingerprint of it and cannot show it again. If it is lost, revoke it and make another.

Whoever opens the link sees the object, the claimant community, the place of origin, the current holder, the evidence, the status and its history, and the shared messages. Internal messages, the contact details and the staff notes are not shown. They can write in the dialogue, as the person the link was made for, unless the link is read-only. A revoked, expired or unknown link shows only that it is not active.

## In the public catalogue

Two choices under **Repatriation claims** in the menu decide what the public sees. Both are off until the institution turns them on.

**Show the register of claims.** The public catalogue gains a **Repatriation** page: how many claims there are, where they stand, the places and communities they come from, returns against claims still in dialogue, and the latest claims. The same figures are open data, as JSON. Each claim about a published description has a **virtual return** page, linked from the description: the object in its origin context, where the dialogue stands, and the knowledge people have shared about it. A claim about a description the public catalogue does not show is counted but never shown.

On a virtual return, anyone can **share what they know**: provenance, oral history, a correction, the source community, or something else. Nothing appears until someone who manages claims approves it under **Knowledge to review**. A contributor is named only if they give their name and ask to be credited; otherwise their contribution appears anonymously, and no name is kept.

**Let anyone lodge a claim.** Every published description gains **Lodge a repatriation claim**. The form asks for the claimant community, a contact email, and optionally a name, the place of origin and the grounds. The claimant must agree that it records a request to open a dialogue, not a legal determination. The claim arrives as Registered, marked as lodged by the public, and those who manage claims are told. A hidden field, a short wait before the form may be sent, and a limit of five a minute keep robots out.
