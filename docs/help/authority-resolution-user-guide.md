# Resolving names to authority records and places

A description names people, organisations and places in passing: "a letter from Maria Botha, daughter of Jan Botha, written in Pretoria in 1902". **Names to resolve**, under the staff menu at `/authority`, brings each such name together with the authority records or places of the institution it may be. It ranks them by evidence and keeps what you decide. It is for accounts that may review proposals.

## Where the names come from

Every proposed person, organisation or place is a name to resolve:

- **Find names with AI** on a unit of description proposes the names a model finds (see [Names, summaries and condition from AI](ai-tools-user-guide.md)). These come straight into the queue, with exactly where the model found them.
- Names sent through the API are proposals too. The queue says how many are not in it yet, and **Bring them in** adds them (at most 200 at a time). `php artisan workspace:authority:promote` does the same for all.

## What each name carries

Opening a name shows:

- **where it stands**: the 150 characters before and after it in the unit's description and the transcripts of its pages, and how often it appears;
- **what is near it**: dates, places and other people or organisations named in the same paragraph, and words of role within 120 characters, such as "daughter of", "witnessed by" or "born in";
- **its candidates**: the institution's own authority records of the right kind, or its places, five unless your settings say otherwise.

A candidate is found when its names hold every word of the name, so "Botha, Maria" is found for "Maria Botha". Candidates are ranked by score, and each shows what every kind of evidence said about it. [Scoring the evidence](authority-resolution-evidence-scoring.md) explains how.

## Deciding

- **Link to this one** links the name to a candidate.
- **Link to another** links it to a record or place the Workspace did not rank, by its identifier.
- **Make a new authority record** or **Make a new place** opens a form for a new record, filled as far as it can be (see below).
- **Park it, saying why** sets it aside until there is more to go on.
- **Reject it** says it is not the name of a person, organisation or place. Say why: the reason helps the model learn.

Linking and making a new record accept the proposal in the review queue: the access point is made as for any proposal, and the proposal shows as accepted. When a model found the name, the relation keeps the model's assertion, which your acceptance confirms. The RiC-O export then says it was generated by the model and reviewed. Rejecting rejects the proposal too.

Every decision is kept as it was made, with every candidate you saw and its evidence at that moment. Decisions cannot be changed afterwards. They are listed at the foot of the name's page.

## Making a new record

The form is filled from the outside authorities switched on for the institution. The first in your order wins each field:

- VIAF and Wikidata for people and organisations;
- Wikidata and GeoNames for places.

Each field says where it came from: an authority with its address and licence, the name as found, what was proposed, or the passage it was found in. Check every field. What came from where is kept with the new record, and an authority record's sources (ISAAR(CPF) 5.4.8) list the authorities it was filled from.

As ISAAR(CPF) asks, a new authority record needs its authorised form of name, dates of existence and history. A new place needs a name, and either both a latitude and a longitude, or neither.

## Parked names

**Parked names** lists them with the reason, who parked them and when. Each night the Workspace looks for their candidates again (`php artisan workspace:authority:scan-parked`) and flags a name when a candidate has appeared that was not there before. **Take it up again** returns a name to the queue, with its candidates found afresh.

## Giving names to a colleague

Tick names in the queue, or use **Give it to** on a name's page, to give them to another archivist who may review proposals, with a note. They are told by e-mail, and **Given to me** in the queue shows them theirs.

## Settings

Under **Admin**, then **Resolving names**, whoever administers the institution sets the following:

- **the candidates a name is given**;
- **the words of role**: Heratio's English list with Afrikaans beside it, one a line in five kinds (kinship, witness, location, movement, other);
- **the outside authorities**: each is on or off, with its order, its calls a minute and how long its answers are kept.

GeoNames needs your own registered username. All the outside authorities are off until switched on, and the name searched for is sent to them. Getty TGN, GND, ISNI and SAGNC are listed but not yet searched, as in Heratio.

## On the server

- `workspace:authority:rescore` finds and scores the candidates of every waiting name again.
- `workspace:authority:export-feedback --format=jsonl` (or `conll`) writes the rejected names not yet exported, for training the named-entity model.
- `workspace:authority:cache-clear` removes the cached answers of outside authorities.
- `workspace:authority:status` counts the names in each state.

## Compared with Heratio

In Heratio a mention is a row of its NER table. In the Workspace it is a proposal of the review queue, so a name sent through the API is resolved the same way and the two can never disagree. The other differences:

- Candidates are found by every word of a name, in any of a record's names, rather than by the whole name in the authorised form.
- The evidence is read from the RiC description: an agent's dates, its relations to places and other agents, and its history.
- A place's own dates count against it, where Heratio's place conflict check is a stub.
- Decisions are kept in the database and exported with the relations, rather than written to a separate Fuseki graph.
